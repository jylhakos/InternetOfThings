# Clouds

  Cloud computing involves obtaining computing resources over the internet.

- Infrastructure as a Service (IaaS)

  IaaS can be thought of as leasing virtual computing resources online, where you take charge of managing those resources yourself.

- Platform as a Service (Paas)
  
  PaaS offers you services and a platform that manages the underlying infrastructure on your behalf, making you dependent on the platform provider.

- Software as a Service (SaaS)

  SaaS can be understood as a subscription model where the service provider takes care of everything, from the infrastructure to the cloud computing aspects.

### Deploying and managing applications on the cloud.

  Once you have determined the necessary cloud infrastructure, such as IaaS, the next important factor to consider is the provider of that cloud service.

### Scalability, availability and resilience

Scalability is the ability of your application to adapt and scale your computing resources in response to increasing demand for those resources.

Vertical scaling refers to increasing the resources of one instance of your application.

Horizontal scaling is the preferred method for achieving scalability, as it involves adding more instances of your application to manage the increased computational load.

Availability refers to your application’s capability to remain accessible to users.

Resilience refers to the capacity of your application to recover back from failures.

## AWS

Amazon Web Services (AWS) offers a vast array of services and a worldwide presence, providing high customization options that may introduce some complexity.

## Azure

Microsoft Azure is an superb choice for organizations already using Microsoft products, offering smooth integration with Windows Server, Active Directory, and Office tools.

## GCP

Google Cloud Platform (GCP) is preferred by teams engaged in data engineering, machine learning, and containerized applications, and it excels in analytics.
